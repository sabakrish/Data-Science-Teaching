# Generic Undergraduate Syllabus: Data Science Theory and Statistical Learning

**Level:** Upper-year undergraduate  
**Indicative duration:** 12 teaching weeks  
**Prerequisite assumptions:** Probability, introductory statistical inference, linear algebra, and programming

## Course description
This course develops conceptual and mathematical understanding of data analysis and statistical learning. It connects sampling and inference with predictive modelling, model complexity, validation, matrix methods, and responsible use of data.

## Learning outcomes
Students should be able to:
1. Explain the roles of study design, data collection, sampling, and measurement.
2. Distinguish descriptive, inferential, predictive, and causal questions.
3. Explain estimation, uncertainty, hypothesis testing, and multiple comparisons.
4. Derive or explain key ideas behind linear and logistic regression.
5. Explain training error, test error, overfitting, generalization, and bias–variance.
6. Explain cross-validation and bootstrap and recognize when they may fail.
7. Describe algorithmic principles of nearest neighbours, trees, ensembles, clustering, and PCA.
8. Explain how matrix decomposition supports dimension reduction.
9. Evaluate the quality and limitations of statistical communication.
10. Apply ethical, privacy-aware, and reproducible analytical practice.

## Indicative weekly schedule
| Week | Topics | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Questions, study design, measurement, data provenance | Study-design critique |
| 2 | Probability, sampling, variability and uncertainty | Concept exercises |
| 3 | Estimation, confidence intervals and hypothesis testing | Inference lab |
| 4 | Regression: assumptions, least squares and interpretation | Derivation and coding |
| 5 | Classification, probabilities and decision rules | Model interpretation |
| 6 | Loss functions, training/test error, generalization | Conceptual comparison |
| 7 | Bias–variance trade-off and model complexity | Simulation |
| 8 | Cross-validation, bootstrap and uncertainty in performance | Resampling notebook |
| 9 | Trees, random forests, bagging and boosting | Algorithm comparison |
| 10 | Clustering, distance, PCA and SVD | Geometry and computation |
| 11 | Multiple testing, reproducibility, data ethics and visual integrity | Critical review |
| 12 | Integrated case study and scientific communication | Final report/presentation |

## Assessment blueprint (adaptable)
- Mathematical/conceptual problem sets: 25%
- Simulation and coding notebooks: 20%
- Critical review of an analytical claim: 15%
- Midterm assessment: 15%
- Final case study and scientific report: 25%

The schedule is a proposed generic syllabus, not an official course outline.
